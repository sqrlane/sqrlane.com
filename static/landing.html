The gap

Everyone stops one step short.

A forwarder's operations desk watches for disruptions and reacts to them by hand. The tools sold into that desk each own one half of the job and hand the other half back to a person at 7am.

01

Risk platforms

Everstream, Interos, Resilinc. Broad, credible monitoring that surfaces the event and scores it. Then it is your problem.

Stops at: the alert

02

Execution tools

TMS and visibility stacks move the booking once someone has already decided it should move. They do not watch the world.

Starts at: the decision already made

03

Lanewatch

Reads the event, triages the book, makes the call per shipment, explains it, and writes the two emails that call requires.

Closes: risk → reroute → comms

The two halves are also priced for enterprise. The desk this is modelled on — a Head of Operations at a mid-size DACH or Benelux forwarder — sits below that line and does the joining by hand.

How it works

Three agents, one closed loop.

No framework, no scheduler, no queue. Plain functions calling functions over shared JSON state — which is exactly why you can read what happened afterwards.

01

Risk Monitor Live

Pulls GDELT, six RSS feeds and three Rhine gauges, then has the model classify each item for logistics relevance — chokepoint, disruption type, severity. Numbers from the gauges are read by threshold, not by the model. Whatever has not answered inside the budget is marked skipped and the cycle moves on.

25s hard budget for the whole pull 8s per-request timeout Seven chokepoints: HAM · RTM · ANR · SUEZ · REDSEA · COGH · RHINE
02

Route Advisor Decides

Maps each shipment's route against active risk. Code computes the facts — which chokepoints the lane touches, added transit days, whether the schedule slack absorbs them, the revised ETA. The model weighs slack against added transit against expected disruption delay, makes the call, and explains it in plain language.

Arithmetic and dates never go to the model A route it wasn't offered is refused and downgraded to hold Clean lanes short-circuit with no model call
03

Comms Agent Drafts only

Two separate calls, two different voices. A carrier email is a transaction between operators, so route codes and chokepoint ids are fine in it. A customer email is a relationship, so internal vocabulary appearing there is flagged as a warning. Both come back as text and stop there.

Every draft carries status: DRAFT — not sent Nothing leaves the process
Differentiator one

The wires are not first.

A disruption is known locally long before it is news globally. The union announces the walkout; the regional broadcaster carries it; the port's own community talks about it — and only then does an international wire pick it up, often the better part of a day later. A monitor that watches the wires is structurally late, not because it is bad, but because it is reading downstream.

So Lanewatch reads the sources themselves — local outlets, regional broadcasters, trade press, government gauges — across every region its lanes pass through. Somewhere in the world one of them is publishing right now, whatever the hour here.

  • At source Local sources are read directly, not picked up second-hand from a wire
  • Coverage Sources spanning the North Range, the Low Countries, the Red Sea and Gulf, and the Western Mediterranean — every corridor on the board
  • Evidence Every event shows the outlets that carried it and how far ahead of the wires they were, so the claim is checkable rather than asserted

Differentiator two

The hard one is the interesting one.

Two of the five shipments reroute cleanly and two are never touched — the system does not cry wolf. The one worth reading is SHP-002, where there is no good option and the job is to pick the least-bad one and say why.

Two 40ft reefers of temperature-controlled pharma, one day of slack, and a final customer who is in Hamburg. Rerouting via Rotterdam adds road transit and a cold-chain transfer — and every transfer is a GDP-compliance event. So the call is to hold, notify, and be explicit that the deadline is at risk.

  • Trail Every decision keeps a reasoning trail — including the guard rails that fired
  • Fallback If the provider is unreachable, the fallback is transparent rules and the card is badged rule — never a silent downgrade
  • Model split A strip on the board names the model in use and the model-versus-rules split, so the central claim is checkable at a glance
SHP-002 · Reefer pharma · Ningbo → Hamburg Hold + notify
  • Route touches an active chokepoint. R-HAM-STD discharges at HAM. EVT-HAM-STRIKE is active there at severity high, 3–5 days expected delay.
  • No alternate route on file. The only Hamburg-discharging alternate is the Cape routing at 42 days — ten days longer against one day of slack.
  • Rotterdam was considered and rejected. Discharge at RTM adds road transit to Hamburg plus one reefer transfer. The consignee is in Hamburg, so the reroute moves the box away from where it needs to be.
  • Cold chain is the binding constraint. +2 to +8 °C, continuous power. Each transfer is a break risk and a GDP-compliance event; the strike costs days, a temperature excursion costs the consignment.
  • Decision: hold at origin, notify now. Deadline is likely breached either way. Holding keeps the cold chain unbroken and buys the customer the time to react.

The last mile

It writes the emails. It does not send them.

A decision that lands in a dashboard is still work for a person. Every actioned shipment comes back with the two emails that decision requires — one to the carrier, one to the customer — waiting behind an approval gate.

Draft — not sent No internal codes

Human-in-the-loop is the design, not a missing feature.

There is no SMTP, no email library and no transport of any kind imported anywhere in the codebase, and a test parses every file in src/ on each run to keep it that way. Approval flips a state in the browser; there is nothing for it to trigger.

A disruption is not one email. It is a day of them.

The carrier sends a notice. The customer asks where their box is. A rate request lands on the same lane you are busy re-planning. Deciding is the interesting half; this is the half that actually eats the day.

No TMS is contacted. There is no client, no credential and no endpoint — a write-back is a described change, and it stays one.

Everything outbound sits behind the same gate. A drafted reply, a drafted quote and a queued write-back are all actions that leave the building, so all three wait on a person exactly as the carrier and customer emails do — DRAFT - not sent and QUEUED - not written. The test that proves the emails cannot be sent now checks these too.

What it reads

Free, keyless, and few on purpose.

Every extra source is one more thing that can break live in front of an audience. Three source families carry the whole story, each behind its own small function so one can be pulled without touching the others.

SourceRoleKey
GDELT DOC 2.0 7 queries Global news backbone, refreshing about every 15 minutes. Queries are pinned per corridor — North Range, Low Countries, Red Sea and Gulf, Western Mediterranean — so each one watches the regional press for that lane. None
RSS 10 feeds Regional broadcasters and trade press close to the lanes — NDR Hamburg, tagesschau, DW, NOS, RTVE, France Info, Le Monde, Al Jazeera and gCaptain. This is where the earliness edge actually lives; the wire feeds are kept so the lead over them is measurable rather than asserted. None
PEGELONLINE 3 gauges Rhine water levels at Kaub, Duisburg-Ruhrort and Emmerich, mapped to the RHINE chokepoint. Read by threshold, not by the model — they are numbers, not prose. None

Deliberately not wired: anything paid, and anything optional. Reuters is named in the research but retired its public RSS feeds, so it is left out — a dead feed is a live failure.

Scope

What it deliberately does not do.

Scope creep is the failure mode for a prototype like this. These are not roadmap items being coy — they are out, by decision.

No route optimisation

Routes are pre-authored candidates. The agent chooses among them and justifies the choice; it never computes a route.

No sending

Emails are drafted and displayed. No transport of any kind is imported anywhere in the codebase, and a test enforces it.

No TMS integration

The shipments are synthetic and authored so one disruption yields three different, defensible decisions.

No scheduler, no database

Button-triggered, JSON files, in memory. For a demo, magic on command beats a background job.

Live news pull, classification, decisions, drafts Synthetic the five shipments and the injected Hamburg strike

The injected strike uses the same event format as a live one, so it flows through the real pipeline rather than around it. That is what makes the demo honest and repeatable at the same time — and it is why the Risk Monitor stays genuinely live even though everything downstream runs on authored data.

This is a strong learning artifact and a compelling demo. It is not a live product — the gap between the two is the integration, trust and liability wall, which is real work for later.
