The gap

Everyone stops one step short.

The desk watches for disruptions and reacts to them by hand. Every tool sold into it owns one half of the job and hands the other half back to a person.

01

Risk platforms

Everstream, Interos, Resilinc. They surface the event and score it. Then it is your problem.

Stops at: the alert

02

Execution tools

The TMS and the visibility stack move the booking once someone has decided it should move. They do not watch the world, and they do not decide.

Starts at: the decision already made

03

Lanewatch

Sits on the TMS you already run. Reads the event, triages the book, decides per booking, explains it, drafts the emails, and writes the change back onto the record.

Closes: risk → reroute → comms → the TMS

Both halves are priced for enterprise. The desk this is modelled on — a mid-size DACH or Benelux forwarder — sits below that line and joins them by hand.

How it works

Three agents, one closed loop — run on your TMS.

The bookings come out of the system of record, and every decision goes back into it. No framework, no scheduler, no queue — plain functions over shared JSON, which is why you can read what happened afterwards.

00

TMS link Demo connector

The book is not ours. Every booking is read out of the TMS through one connector — reference, commodity, ports, routing code, ETA, required-by, temperature regime. One door in, so the day it points at a real TMS the whole board follows.

One read path — nothing else opens the book The demo's records are synthetic, the mapping is not
01

Risk Monitor Live

Pulls GDELT, ten RSS feeds and three Rhine gauges, then has the model classify each item — chokepoint, disruption type, severity. Gauges are read by threshold, not by the model. Anything that has not answered inside the budget is skipped and the cycle moves on.

25s hard budget for the whole pull 8s per-request timeout Seven chokepoints: HAM · RTM · ANR · SUEZ · REDSEA · COGH · RHINE
02

Route Advisor Decides

Code computes the facts — which chokepoints the lane touches, added transit days, whether the slack absorbs them, the revised ETA. The model weighs slack against added transit against expected delay, makes the call, and explains it in plain language.

Arithmetic and dates never go to the model A route it wasn't offered is refused and downgraded to hold Clean lanes short-circuit with no model call
03

Comms Agent Drafts only

Two calls, two voices. A carrier email is a transaction between operators, so route codes and chokepoint ids are fine in it. A customer email is a relationship, so internal vocabulary there is flagged. Both come back as text and stop there.

Every draft carries status: DRAFT — not sent Nothing leaves the process
04

Back into the TMS Demo connector

A decision nobody records is a decision nobody acts on. Risk flags the exception on the booking. Routing changes discharge port, routing code, ETA and status. Comms files each draft on the booking's communication log. A booking left on plan writes nothing at all.

Every change is QUEUED — not written No client, no credential, no endpoint A person approves the write
Differentiator one

The wires are not first.

A disruption is known locally long before it is news globally. The union announces the walkout, the regional broadcaster carries it, and a wire picks it up the better part of a day later. A monitor watching the wires is late because it is reading downstream.

Lanewatch reads those sources directly — local outlets, regional broadcasters, trade press, government gauges — across every region its lanes cross. Whatever the hour, one of them is publishing.

  • At source Local sources are read directly, not second-hand from a wire
  • Coverage The North Range, the Low Countries, the Red Sea and Gulf, the Western Mediterranean — every corridor on the board
  • Evidence Every event shows which outlets carried it and how far ahead of the wires — checkable, not asserted

Re-mode · R-RTM-RAIL

Revised ETA Sep 18 · original Sep 17 · required by Sep 20

Rail and road out of Rotterdam book ten to fourteen days ahead, so the slot has to be taken before the queue forms. Cost index 100 → 114. The sea leg and the discharge port are untouched — only the inland leg moves, which is why this is a re-mode and not a reroute.

What the advisor weighed

  • Three days of slack against a one-day penalty — it fits.
  • The active risk sits on the barge leg. The sea leg and Rotterdam are clean, so a discharge-port change buys nothing.
  • R-COGH-BSL was on the table and refused — 49 days, far outside the slack.

SHP-006 is the only Rhine-barge leg on the board, so the other six stay on plan. The system does not cry wolf.

Differentiator two

The hard one is the interesting one.

Two of the seven reroute cleanly and four are never touched. The one worth reading is SHP-002, where there is no good option and the job is to pick the least-bad one and say why.

Two 40ft reefers of pharma, one day of slack, and a customer who is in Hamburg. Rerouting via Rotterdam adds road transit and a cold-chain transfer, and every transfer is a GDP-compliance event. So: hold, notify, and say plainly that the deadline is at risk.

  • Trail Every decision keeps a reasoning trail — including the guard rails that fired
  • Fallback If the provider is unreachable it falls back to rules and the card is badged rule — never a silent downgrade
  • Model split A strip on the board names the model in use and the model-versus-rules split — checkable at a glance
SHP-002 · Reefer pharma · Ningbo → Hamburg Hold + notify
  • Route touches an active chokepoint. R-HAM-STD discharges at HAM. EVT-HAM-STRIKE is active there at severity high, 3–5 days expected delay.
  • No alternate route on file. The only Hamburg-discharging alternate is the Cape routing at 42 days — ten days longer against one day of slack.
  • Rotterdam was considered and rejected. Discharge at RTM adds road transit to Hamburg plus one reefer transfer. The consignee is in Hamburg, so the reroute moves the box away from where it needs to be.
  • Cold chain is the binding constraint. +2 to +8 °C, continuous power. Each transfer is a break risk and a GDP-compliance event; the strike costs days, a temperature excursion costs the consignment.
  • Decision: hold at origin, notify now. Deadline is likely breached either way. Holding keeps the cold chain unbroken and buys the customer the time to react.

The last mile

It writes the emails. It does not send them.

A decision that lands in a dashboard is still work for a person. Every actioned shipment comes back with both emails it needs — carrier and customer — behind an approval gate.

Draft — not sent No internal codes

Human-in-the-loop is the design, not a missing feature.

No SMTP, no email library, no transport of any kind in the codebase — a test re-parses every file in src/ on each run. Approval flips a state in the browser; there is nothing for it to trigger.

The rest of the desk

A disruption is not one email. It is a day of them.

The carrier sends a notice. The customer asks where their box is. A rate request lands on the lane you are re-planning. And until someone types it in, the TMS still says the old discharge port. Deciding is the interesting half. This is the half that eats the day — all of it on the same booking record.

What it reads

Free, keyless, and few on purpose.

Every extra source is one more thing that can break in front of an audience. Three families carry the whole story, each behind its own function so one can be pulled without touching the others.

SourceRoleKey
GDELT DOC 2.0 7 queries Global news backbone, refreshing about every 15 minutes. One query per corridor — North Range, Low Countries, Red Sea and Gulf, Western Mediterranean — each watching the regional press for that lane. None
RSS 10 feeds Regional broadcasters and trade press close to the lanes — NDR Hamburg, tagesschau, DW, NOS, RTVE, France Info, Le Monde, Al Jazeera, gCaptain. This is where the earliness edge lives. The wire feeds stay so the lead over them is measurable, not asserted. None
PEGELONLINE 3 gauges Rhine water levels at Kaub, Duisburg-Ruhrort and Emmerich, mapped to the RHINE chokepoint. Read by threshold, not by the model — numbers, not prose. None
Runtime model Groq Resolved at runtime against the provider's own model list, never hard-coded — a retired model name returns a 404 that looks exactly like a broken key. Swappable in one file. Yours

Not wired, deliberately: anything paid, anything optional. Reuters retired its public RSS feeds, so it is out — a dead feed is a live failure.

Scope

What it deliberately does not do.

Scope creep is the failure mode for a prototype. These are not roadmap items being coy — they are out, by decision.

No route optimisation

Routes are pre-authored candidates. The agent chooses among them and justifies the choice; it never computes a route.

No sending

Emails are drafted and displayed. No transport of any kind is imported anywhere in the codebase, and a test enforces it.

No live TMS connection — yet

Working through the TMS is the design, and the connector models both directions. What is missing is the far end: no vendor, no credential, no endpoint. The bookings behind it are synthetic, authored so one disruption yields three different decisions.

No scheduler, no database

Button-triggered, JSON files, in memory. For a demo, magic on command beats a background job.

A demo, not a live product — the wall between the two is integration and trust, and this connector stops at it.
