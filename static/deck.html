01  What The status quo is manual, and it is expensive

European freight forwarding still runs on people doing the data work by hand.

€3.4bn a year
the annual labour value of the work being done by hand across the European market.
SQRlane analysis

No product problem

The software to run a forwarding desk exists. Buying more of it does not remove the hours, because the hours are not going into the software. They are going into moving information towards it.

A labour problem nobody has priced

We are not displacing a competing tool. We are displacing spreadsheets and hours — a harder sale to start, and a far larger one to finish.

Every hour on the next four slides is someone carrying information towards a record, by hand, because nothing else will.

01  What Where the hours actually go

Sixty percent of the day is spent carrying information, not deciding anything.

60%
of the working day goes to coordination — chasing status, searching for information, switching between apps. Only 40% goes to the skilled work.
Asana, Anatomy of Work Index 2022

The five things that eat it

  • Finding it. Which app, whose thread, which version.
  • Retyping it. Into the system, by hand.
  • Deciding what is true. Three places, three versions.
  • Switching. Around 25 times a day, across ten apps.
  • One person holding it. They take leave and the shipment stalls.

Coordination that needs judgement — negotiating a rate, deciding a reroute — is what the forwarder sells. Coordination that only carries information from one place to another is not. We take the second.

The third one is the only one that costs money rather than time. A surcharge agreed in a chat that never reaches the invoice is paid by the forwarder.

01  What Who has it, and why it persists

Nobody has fixed it because hiring works.

01

The substitute is headcount, not software

They add people when volume grows, because there is nothing on the market that does the work.

02

There is often nothing to integrate with

Most forwarders have no system of record to connect to. And the channels the work lives in are conversations, not systems — so nobody connected them.

03

So cost scales with volume

Winning a bigger customer means hiring against it. The margin problem gets worse exactly when the business gets better.

That last line is the whole problem. It is not an efficiency story — it is a ceiling on the business, and it is the version a finance director acts on.

02  Why The gap — two mature categories, and a person between them

One side alerts. The other side executes. Nobody closes the distance.

Category one — risk

Supply-chain risk platforms

Everstream Analytics, Interos, Resilinc, Prewave.

  • They stop at the alert. They do not decide, and they never touch a booking.
  • They mostly read the same wires as each other.
  • Enterprise-priced — out of reach for the mid-market.
The gap

The desk

A person reads the alert, works out which bookings it touches, decides reroute or hold, writes two mails, and re-keys the consequence into the TMS — one booking at a time.

This is the manual bridge. It is where the hours go, and it is what SQRlane is.

Category two — execution

TMS & visibility platforms

CargoWise, Riege Scope, Descartes, Transporeon, project44.

  • They hold the booking and move it — after someone has decided it should move.
  • They do not watch the world.
  • Deeply embedded, and staying. Not a rip-and-replace target.

Named for positioning only. None of these systems is connected to SQRlane today — no vendor, no credential, no endpoint, and nothing is ever written. The TMS layer in the current build is a demo connector.

02  Why Why now, and what holds

Three things became true at once.

01

Disruption stopped being episodic

Red Sea diversions, Rhine low water, port strikes, tariff filings. A desk that used to absorb one bad quarter a year now absorbs one a month.

02

Reasoning got cheap enough to run per booking

Per-token inference means a judgement call on every shipment on the board costs cents, not a data-science team. Three years ago this product was not affordable.

03

The TMS finally has a door

Modern forwarding systems expose the booking over an API. The decision can now be written back onto the record instead of typed onto it.

What holds, once we are in

The write-back is the moat

Alerting is commoditised. Being trusted to change a booking is not — it is earned per account, over months of approved decisions, and it does not transfer.

The reasoning trail compounds

Every decision records why it was made and whether a human approved it. That history is the customer’s, it lives on their bookings, and it makes the next decision better.

Breadth is hard to copy cheaply

Forty-two free sources across six families, read together on every run. Any one is easy. Reading all of them, concurrently, without one slow source stalling the cycle, is the engineering.

We do not claim to be first to alerting — that category is a decade old and crowded. The claim is narrower and more defensible: first to carry the decision all the way back onto the booking.

03  How The loop

It opens and closes in the same place: the booking.

The bookings are read out of the system of record, the agents decide against those records, and every action is written back onto them. Not a second book to keep. Not a dashboard beside the TMS. The same record, all the way round.

03  How What it watches

Not a news monitor. Forty-two free, keyless sources in six families, read together on every run.

31
News — regional broadcasters on the corridors’ doorsteps, the international wires, and the trade press.
3
River gauges — water levels on the inland waterways a barge leg depends on.
3
Weather & sea state — gusts over the crane, wave height on the corridor.
2
Seismic & natural hazards — each reading mapped to the nearest chokepoint, or dropped.
2
Government filings — tariff, sanctions, customs and port-security registers.
1
Reference rates — the rate a reroute is actually billed at.

Prose goes to the model. Numbers never do.

A gust, a wave height, a magnitude and a water level are classified by a threshold. It is cheaper, it is repeatable, and it cannot hallucinate a severity.

Nothing is load-bearing

Every source is its own small function inside a shared budget, and the families are read concurrently. A source that is down, slow or reshaped is reported failed and skipped — it cannot stall the cycle.

Reading close to the event is why a disruption often lands here before the international wires carry it. Reading widely is why it lands here at all when it never becomes a headline.

03  How Scope — what the agents actually do

One desk, covered. Every output is a change to a booking.

Running today

Risk · Routing · Comms

  • Risk — watches all six families, classifies what is relevant to a lane.
  • Routing — weighs schedule slack against added transit and expected delay; reroute, hold or on plan, with the reasoning recorded.
  • Comms — drafts the carrier mail and the customer mail in separate voices.
Commercial

RFQ · Quoting · Rate

  • Inbound rate request read into structured fields.
  • Priced against the lane, including the surcharge a live disruption adds.
  • Answered with a drafted quote, on the booking.
Inbound

Inbox · Documents

  • Carrier and customer mail triaged: intent classified, linked to the booking.
  • A reply drafted where one is warranted — and none where it is not.
  • Documents read, checked and filed against the record.
Execution

Booking · Milestones

  • The amendment a decision forces: a reroute is a change of discharge port, a hold is a hold at the load port.
  • Milestones tracked and pushed onto the record.
Finance & compliance

Invoice · Customs

  • Carrier invoice reconciled against the rate agreed — the surcharge that was never quoted is the finding.
  • A reroute moves the country of entry, so the entry, the EORI and the clearance agent change. It escalates rather than files.
The gate

Nothing sends. Nothing writes.

Every mail is DRAFT — not sent. Every record change is QUEUED — not written. One approval queue, grouped by booking, so a person signs off exactly what they can see.
